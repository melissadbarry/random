🚀 6-Week Fast Track 15 min/day

Class moves slowly because a group has to move slowly. This doesn't. 15 focused minutes a day, on top of class (not instead of it), is enough to actually move — daily reps beat weekly ones every time. A simple loop for each day:

  • ~5 min — Hear it: Cheat Sheet, Class Notes, or a Sentence Builder reference grid. Tap every 🔊, say it back out loud.
  • ~5 min — Use it: one Sentence Builder drill, Crash Course day, or Class Notes quiz.
  • ~5 min — Live it: say it to a real person or screen — your Uber driver, a barista, or shadow a line from Portuguese TV.

⏳ Verbs: Present, Past & Future the easy way

The 20 verbs that carry most day-to-day conversation. Only the three tenses you actually need, using the simplest form Brazilians really say. Tap a verb to open it.

  • Present — what you do / are doing now: Eu falo português.
  • Past — finished actions (pretérito perfeito), the one past tense to learn first: Eu falei com ele.
  • Future — the easy version everyone actually uses: vou + verb, never changing the verb: Eu vou falar.

🌴 São Paulo Travel Guide Language + Culture

🚇 Getting Around SP

  • The Metrô is fast, cheap, and the best way to skip traffic — load a Bilhete Único card.
  • Uber and 99 are widely used and generally safer than hailing a street taxi at night.
  • Trânsito (traffic) is heavy most of the day — always build in extra time.
  • Neighborhoods worth knowing: Jardins (upscale), Vila Madalena (bohemian nightlife), Pinheiros (trendy cafés & bars), Itaim Bibi (business), Vila Mariana (quiet, residential, right by Ibirapuera Park), Av. Paulista (museums, closed to cars on Sundays for walking/biking).

☕ Ordering Food & Coffee

  • A "cafézinho" (small strong coffee) is often offered as a gesture of hospitality — say yes.
  • Padarias (bakeries) are everywhere for a quick pão de queijo, coxinha, or fresh juice.
  • Rodízio means all-you-can-eat, usually churrascaria (meat) or pizza.
  • For an affordable local lunch, look for "prato feito" or "comida a kilo" (pay-by-weight buffets).
  • A 10% service charge is often already included — check before adding extra.

🗣️ Local Slang (São Paulo)

🤝 Cultural Tips

  • Greetings are warm: a cheek kiss is common between women/mixed pairs, handshakes or hugs among men — follow their lead.
  • Brazilians stand close and speak expressively — that warmth isn't aggression.
  • Punctuality is relaxed for social plans, but stricter for business meetings.
  • Small talk before business is the norm — build rapport before getting to the point.
  • Genuine compliments are common — accept them graciously.
  • Ease into politics and religion with new acquaintances — get to know people first.

⚠️ Avoid Awkward Moments

  • Don't default to Spanish — Brazilians speak Portuguese, and mixing them up is a common (and noticed) mistake.
  • Skip the "OK" 👌 hand sign — it's considered rude/vulgar in Brazil.
  • The check won't come automatically — ask for "a conta, por favor."
  • Tipping ~10% is customary, even when a service charge appears to be included.
  • Stick to filtered or bottled water when you're unsure.

🗣️ Language Exchange & Social
Meetup.com

🌐 Intercâmbio / Language Exchange Nights

São Paulo has a large, active language-exchange scene — casual meetups where Portuguese and English speakers pair up and swap languages over drinks or coffee.

How to find one: search Meetup for "intercâmbio," "language exchange," or "polyglot" groups filtered to São Paulo.
Apps

📱 Tandem / HelloTalk

Language-exchange apps often list local in-person meetups organized by their community — a low-pressure way to meet people before showing up solo.

🎤 Skills Growth & Business Networking
Toastmasters International

🎤 Toastmasters

A public-speaking and leadership club with chapters worldwide, including several in São Paulo — some run in English, some in Portuguese. Great for building speaking confidence in either language while meeting professionals.

How to find one: use the "Find a Club" search on toastmasters.org and filter by São Paulo.
InterNations

🌍 InterNations São Paulo

A global expat and international-professional network with an active São Paulo community — mixers, activity groups, and professional networking events.

How to find one: check internations.org for upcoming São Paulo events (membership may be required to RSVP).

📍 Vila Mariana Neighborhood Spotlight

A quiet, walkable, residential pocket of SP right next to Ibirapuera Park — good home base energy.

🏊

SESC Vila Mariana

A well-loved cultural & sports center with a pool, theater, library, and exhibitions — affordable membership and a great source of classes and local events.

🌳

Ibirapuera Park (East/South Gates)

Vila Mariana sits right next to Ibirapuera's eastern edges — some of the quickest access points for a morning run, picnic, or weekend fitness class.

🛍️

Rua Domingos de Morais

The neighborhood's main commercial street — shops, bakeries, pharmacies, and casual bites for everyday errands.

☕

Quiet, Walkable Streets

Tree-lined residential blocks make Vila Mariana one of the more laid-back, walkable corners of SP — good for slow mornings and café-hopping.

🚇

Easy Metro Access

Vila Mariana, Ana Rosa & Santa Cruz stations (Blue Line) put Av. Paulista and downtown just a few stops away.
